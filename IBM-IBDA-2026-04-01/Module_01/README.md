## Overview

This module sets the **foundation** for the whole course. Before any Python, SQL, or Excel, you build a **mental map** of the data analytics landscape: what a data analyst actually does, where data lives, how it moves from raw source to insight, and how analysts communicate what they find. It is mostly **concepts and viewpoints**—short videos and readings—closed out by one **hands-on reflection activity**.

The core takeaway:

> Before you can analyze data, you need to understand the ecosystem you're working in. This module gives you the **vocabulary, roles, and workflow** of data analytics so the technical modules that follow have somewhere to land.

---

## Why Start With the Big Picture

It's tempting to jump straight to tools. But analysts who understand the *why* and *where* make better decisions about the *how*:

* **Roles and ecosystem** — Knowing the players (engineers, analysts, scientists) and where data is stored tells you who to ask and where to look
* **The data workflow** — Identify → gather → wrangle → clean → analyze → communicate is the spine that every later module plugs into
* **Communication** — A correct analysis nobody understands changes nothing; storytelling and visualization are part of the job, not an afterthought

This module answers the first real question of the career: *What is this work, and what does the path through it look like?*

## What This Module Covers

The content follows the course's four units, moving from the role itself through the ecosystem, the end-to-end workflow, and finally careers.

### Unit 1 — The Data Ecosystem and the Data Analyst

* Overview of the **data analyst ecosystem** and **data repositories**

* **Sources of data**; **data marts, data lakes, ETL, and data pipelines**

### Unit 3 — Gathering, Wrangling, and Communicating Data

* **Identifying** data for analysis and locating **data sources**

### Unit 4 — Careers in Data Analytics

* **Career opportunities** and the **many paths** into the profession
* Viewpoints: getting into the field, what employers look for, career options, advice for aspiring analysts, and women in data professions

## Key Takeaways

* **Data analytics is an ecosystem**, not a single tool—roles, repositories, and pipelines all play a part.
* The **analyst workflow** is identify → gather → wrangle → clean → analyze → communicate; later modules each deepen a piece of it.
* Data lives in many places (**RDBMS, NoSQL, files, data lakes**) and arrives via **ETL and pipelines**.
* **Big data** brings its own foundations and processing tools worth knowing by name early.
* **Communication and storytelling** are core analyst skills, not optional extras.
* There are **many paths** into the profession; the module closes by mapping them.

## Your Map of the Data Analytics Landscape

Think of this module as drawing the **map** you'll navigate for the rest of the course.

You don't need to memorize every term or tool name here. The goal is to **recognize the roles, the data sources, the end-to-end workflow, and the importance of communication**—so that when you pick up Excel, Python, and SQL in the modules ahead, you already know *why* you're learning them and *where* they fit.
