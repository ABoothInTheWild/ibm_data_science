## Overview

This module introduces the **foundational ideas behind data science methodology**.
Rather than focusing on tools or algorithms, Module 1 emphasizes **how data scientists think**, structure problems, and make decisions using data.

The core takeaway:
> Data science is a disciplined, iterative process for solving real-world problems with data.

---

## What Data Science Really Is

Data science is not just about models or code. It is a **structured problem-solving framework** that integrates:

* Business or domain understanding
* Statistical and analytical reasoning
* Data collection, understanding, and preparation
* Modeling, evaluation, deployment, and feedback

The **question comes before the data**, and the **problem comes before the solution**.

---

## Why Methodology Matters

A data science methodology provides a **repeatable and defensible approach** to decision-making.

Without a methodology:

* Teams jump to solutions too early
* Models answer the wrong question
* Results are difficult to explain or trust

Methodology ensures that we are solving the **right problem**, using the **right approach**, with the **right data**.

---

## John Rollins’ Data Science Methodology

John Rollins’ framework outlines **10 iterative stages**:

1. **Business Understanding**
   Define the problem, goals, and success criteria.

2. **Analytic Approach**
   Decide how data can answer the question (descriptive, predictive, classification, clustering, etc.).

3. **Data Requirements**
   Identify what data is needed, in what format, and at what level of detail.

4. **Data Collection**
   Gather the data from relevant sources. Missing data is common and expected.

5. **Data Understanding**
   Explore the data using statistics and visualization to assess quality, patterns, and gaps.

6. **Data Preparation**
   Clean, transform, and engineer features. This stage often takes the most time.

7. **Modeling**
   Build models aligned to the chosen analytic approach.

8. **Evaluation**
   Assess whether the model answers the original business question and meets performance needs.

9. **Deployment**
   Put the model into real-world use so stakeholders can act on the results.

10. **Feedback**
    Learn from real-world performance and iterate on the model, data, or problem definition.

These stages are **cyclical and iterative**, not linear.

---

## CRISP-DM Alignment

CRISP-DM (Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining) is a widely adopted industry framework with six stages:

* Business Understanding
* Data Understanding
* Data Preparation
* Modeling
* Evaluation
* Deployment

CRISP-DM closely aligns with John Rollins’ methodology, with **iteration and feedback embedded throughout the process**.

---

## Key Takeaways

* Start with **clarity**, not code
* Data science is an **iterative process**, not a one-time workflow
* Data preparation and understanding often consume **most of the project effort**
* Models only create value when they are **understood, trusted, and used**
* Feedback improves both the model **and** the original problem definition

---

## The Data Scientist Mindset

Think less like a tool user and more like a **problem solver**.

The goal is not to build a model.
The goal is to **make better decisions with data**.
